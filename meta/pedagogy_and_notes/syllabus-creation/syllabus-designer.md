## The 3 Term Phases

### Phase 1: Deconstruction, Electronics & Chindōgu (Weeks 01–05)

- **Week 01**: The Device Art Premise, Night 1 Hook, Hardware Kit Distribution, Introduction to Chindōgu.
- **Week 02**: Electricity Fundamentals, Multimeter Diagnostics, Cardboard Engineering.
- **Week 03**: Microcontroller Programming (Pico & CircuitPython), Digital Input/Output.
- **Week 04**: Sensors (Photocells, Capacitive Touch, Ultrasonic) & Display Output (OLED / NeoPixels).
- **Week 05**: _Project 1 Due_: The "Unuseless" Interactive Device (A fully functional Chindōgu / Anti-Virtual artifact).

### Phase 2: Finite State Machines & Physical Mechanisms (Weeks 06–10)

- **Week 06**: State Machine Logic & Complex Responsive Behaviors.
- **Week 07**: Mechanical Movement, Servos, Sound/Audio Transducers.
- **Week 08**: _Midterm Project Due_: Advanced Mechatronic / Expressive Device Critiques.
- **Week 09**: Casting, Molding & Material Experiments.
- **Week 10**: Final Project Ideation, System Architecture & User Persona Mapping.

### Phase 3: Final Device Fabrication, Exhibition & Documentation (Weeks 11–15)

- **Week 11**: Prototype 1 (Breadboard + Cardboard Enclosure).
- **Week 12**: Prototype 2 (Soldered Circuits + Digital Fabrication / Laser / 3D).
- **Week 13**: Enclosure Refinement & Exploded-View Mechanical Diagrams.
- **Week 14**: Studio Testing & Video Documentation Staging.
- **Week 15**: Final Exhibition, Class Critique & Portfolio Documentation.

## 1. Why Chindōgu and Political Resistance are Two Sides of the Same Coin

At first glance, _Chindōgu_ (whimsical, absurd, "unuseless" tools) and _Political Resistance / Disobedient Objects_ (counter-surveillance, hacktivism, tactical media) seem like opposites: one is playful and humorous, the other serious and confrontational.

In reality, **they share the exact same radical core**:

1. **Rejection of Corporate Utilitarianism**:
   - Commercial tech demands that every device serve consumer efficiency, productivity, data extraction, or capital.
   - _Chindōgu_ rebels against this by creating tools that earnestly solve non-existent problems, short-circuiting consumer logic.
   - _Disobedient Objects_ rebel against this by weaponizing or repurposing everyday tools to resist state/corporate control.
2. **Technological Demystification & Agency**:
   - Both traditions refuse to accept the device as an untouchable commercial "black box."
   - By physically opening, rewiring, modifying, and building custom hardware, students seize **agency** over the physical infosphere.
3. **The Power of the Absurd as Subversion**:
   - As Duncombe (_Cultural Resistance_) and Dunne & Raby (_Critical Design_) note, the most effective resistance often uses irony, humor, and poetic exaggeration to make visible the hidden power dynamics embedded in everyday technology.

## 3. Revised Learning Outcomes (Integrating Art, Politics & Engineering)

1. **Conceptual Critique & Counter-Narratives**: Situate physical computing artifacts within the dual traditions of _Device Art/Chindōgu_ (playful absurdism) and _Disobedient Objects/Design Activism_ (political resistance, tactical media, anti-establishment critique).
2. **Embedded Electronics Engineering**: Safely design, wire, test, and debug mixed-signal electronic circuits using microcontrollers (Raspberry Pi Pico), multimeters, sensors, and power regulation.
3. **Interactive Behavioral Programming**: Architect responsive state machines in CircuitPython that translate physical sensor inputs into mechanical, visual, or acoustic outputs.
4. **Physical Fabrication & Enclosure Craft**: Master rapid physical prototyping across materials—progressing from structural cardboard chassis to precision digital fabrication (laser cutting, 3D printing, casting/molding).
5. **Radical Technical Documentation**: Synthesize rigorous technical documentation (wiring schematics, mechanical drawings, exploded-view diagrams) and compelling video demonstrations communicating the device's political/conceptual intent.
