# Studio Exercise: Speculative Device Mapping in Miro

In this hands-on studio exercise, you will create and diagram a speculative physical device. Rather than designing a standard commercial gadget or phone app, you will design an evocative hardware artifact that responds to a lived human tension in an unfamiliar world.

## 🛠 Step 1: Generate Your Creative Prompt

Run the prompt generator script on your machine to receive your unique combinatorial narrative:

```bash
# Clone or navigate to your local repo:
cd ~/device-art

# Run the prompt generator (outputs your personal 3-layer narrative):
python3 admin/generate_prompts.py
```

*Your output will establish three poetic layers:*
1. **The World**: The atmospheric, dystopian, or utopian reality your user inhabits.
2. **The Human Tension**: What your user is secretly desiring, resisting, or navigating.
3. **The Object Ritual**: The physical constraints and tactile interactions governing your device.

---

## 📋 Step 2: Set Up Your Frame on the Class Miro Board

1. Open our shared **[Miro Board](https://miro.com)**.
2. Claim an empty student frame and label it with your **Preferred Name**.
3. In the center of your frame, create a text card describing your device:
   * **Size & Form Factor**: (e.g. *Handheld wooden oval, pocket-sized brass block, wearable wrist cuff*)
   * **Weight & Material Feel**: (e.g. *Dense cold aluminum, soft textured silicone, raw unfinished walnut*)
   * **Color & Finish**: (e.g. *Matte charcoal with raw copper edges*)
   * **Core Behavior**: A 2–3 sentence overview of how the device functions and transforms the user's situation.

---

## 🧩 Step 3: Map Your Device Architecture (UX & Hardware Gallery)

Using the schematic component library located in the Miro gallery sidebar (or by importing SVGs from [`meta/pedagogy_and_notes/miro_device_canvas/svg_components/`](https://github.com/arielchuri/device-art/tree/main/meta/pedagogy_and_notes/miro_device_canvas/svg_components)), construct your device's interaction and logic flow:

### What to Include in Your Diagram:
1. **User Action / Body Manipulation**: How does the human interact with it?
   * *(e.g. `FLIP / INVERT`, `PRESS / TAP`, `SQUEEZE`, `BLOW / BREATHE`, `RUB / STROKE`, `TURN`)*
2. **Inputs & Sensors**: What physical phenomena does the device measure?
   * *(e.g. `LIGHT SENSOR`, `TILT`, `TOUCH PAD`, `PULSE / EKG`, `AIR QUALITY`, `HALL EFFECT`)*
3. **Internal Logic & Timing**: What happens between input and output?
   * *(e.g. `MICROCONTROLLER`, `DELAY / TIMER`, `DECISION / FORK`, `EDGE AI`, `RTC CLOCK`)*
4. **Outputs & Actuation**: How does the device respond physically to the world?
   * *(e.g. `VIBRATION PULSE`, `HEATING ELEMENT`, `SOLENOID`, `OLED SCREEN`, `BUZZER`, `THERMAL RECEIPT`)*
5. **Connecting Traces**: Connect your components with labeled signal lines (`DIRECT WIRE`, `I2C BUS`, `ONE-WAY SIGNAL`, `WIRELESS LINK`).
6. **User Emotional Shift**: Indicate how the user's emotional state shifts from beginning to end *(e.g. `SECRETIVE` $\to$ `INTRIGUED` $\to$ `NEUTRAL`)*.
